Phonology

Phonology of the Bauhinian language.

Bauhinian features 6 vowels and 26 consonants. May God be kind to me.[1] Also I have successfully explained this language in a way that only Chinese and English bilinguals with a familiarity with the Arabic writing system (for example, me) would be able to get, so if anything's confusing worry not it's not your fault. Note on the letter choices

The script is largely based on the Uyghurche alphabet. I mixed and matched some letters from different languages here. I'm not sure which ones are absent from Arabic since I only learned Uyghurche and not Arabic, but for the ones not present in Uyghurche, ث and ذ are from Arabic, څ is from Pashto, and ڤ is from Kurdish, Swahili, and a number of other languages.[2]

The only letters for which I changed the pronunciation drastically were ق ,ژ, and خ, into Chinese j, q, and x, respectively. This is because I can't find any readily-available equivalent and don't want to add my own little dots while knowing shit.

Writing rules

Bauhinian uses the Arabic script, which is written right-to-left, except for numbers which are left-to-right. I won't go into detail on writing in Arabic script here since there are plenty of other resources for it.

Just know that, like Uyghurche, the letter ئ is used as a vowel separator; and also if a word starts with a vowel, it must be preceded by this letter.

In addition, letters with two dots can be simplified in to a line in handwriting, and three dots can be simplified into a little arrow.

Pronunciation rules

If an ى is at the end of a word, it gets prolonged into an ې.

The letters ق ,ژ, and خ are the most archaic letters, and there are explicit rules about their vowel combinations.

  • When followed by ى or ې, nothing changes and everything is fine.
  • When followed by ا or و, they get pronounced as if there's a ي in between. E.g. 架, 秋.
  • When followed by ۇ, the 乌 turns into 淤. Same thing happens with ي for this point in particular.
  • They cannot be followed by ai or ei.
