## Phonology

Bauhinian featured 5 basic vowels, 3 compound vowels, and 26 consonants. May God be kind to me.[^1]

> [!info]- Note on the letter choices
> The script is largely based on the Uyghurche alphabet. I mixed and matched some letters from different languages here. I'm not sure which ones are absent from Arabic since I only learned Uyghurche and not Arabic, but for the ones not present in Uyghurche, ث and ذ are from Arabic, څ is from Pashto, and ڤ is from Kurdish, Swahili, and a number of other languages.[^2]
>
> The only letters for which I changed the pronunciation drastically were ق , غ, and خ, into Chinese j, q, and x, respectively. This is because I can't find any readily-available equivalent and don't want to add my own little dots while knowing shit.

### Writing rules

Bauhinian uses the Arabic script, which is written right-to-left, except for numbers which are left-to-right. I won't go into detail on writing in Arabic script here since there are plenty of other resources for it.

Just know that, like Uyghurche, the letter ئ is used as a vowel separator; and also if a word starts with a vowel, it must be preceded by this letter.

In addition, letters with two dots can be simplified in to a line in handwriting, and three dots can be simplified into a little arrow.

[^1]: That's actually 31 in total, one fewer than Uyghurche even. But Uyghurche has clear front / back vowel pairings and I don't! Help. So many consonants. Yet I love every one of them dearly and don't want to delete any of them. (It's mostly to flex how many consonants I can pronounce well though.)
[^2]: The beauty of the Arabic script (aside from the fact that it's just really pretty) is how you can just make new letters by adding a shitton of dots to everything without them looking *obviously* extra. One dot, two dot, three dot; could've sworn I saw four-dot variants while digging through font files too. You can technically do the same in Latin with accents, but they just look so obviously added on.
