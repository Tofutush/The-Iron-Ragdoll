The continent, artfully named "the continent," is where all of TIR takes place. But the entire world of TIR isn't much bigger.

The world is a flat rectangle, slightly longer on the north-south axis than on the east-west one. On the edges, the earth crumbles into the void, forming many floating islands of various sizes. The central continent is, in fact, just a giant floating island itself. The corners of the world range from famous tourist attractions (the northwestern one) to unexplored dangers (the southeastern one).

## The corners

### Northwestern

Also called "the End of the World," the northwestern corner is the most famous one for its approachableness and how much of it has developed into tourist attractions. Starcorp owns a fair share of the shops and services, though international law forbade any entity, be it corporations or states, to own any crumbled land. Every country has research outposts here, but of course the biggest ones belong to Bauhinia and Moreland respectively.

As for the activities tourists can do, there are glass bridges between a few islands, and cableways between others where you can ride up in a cart and slide down on a rope. Terrifying stuff but people live for that shit. Some of them.

## Celestial bodies

Which is to say, the Sun and the Moon (always capitalized). The Sun is what gives off light, and the Moon is just a literal rock that reflects it during nighttime. Scientists have speculated that the Sun was a huge condensed mass of void gas (see [next section, Voidspace](#voidspace)) so condensed it started nuclear fusing.

The Sun and the Moon orbit the continent at slightly different speeds, converging on one day where the Moon lands directly in front of the Sun. This happens every 365 days, and is called the New Year, or the Full Eclipse.

The time leading up to the New Year and the time following are called Partial Eclipse, and how much of the Sun is there is called Crescent Sun, Half Sun, Gibbous Sun, and Full Eclipse / New Year. The Eclipses are what mostly determines varying temperatures across seasons — the less the Moon is blocking the Sun, the warmer it is. The Ancient Bauhinians have come up with four seasons and later 24 solar terms to describe the pattern and how it affects agriculture.

The axis around which the sun and the moon rotate is leaned southwards, making it so that the south is always warmer than the north. One of the emperors built two additional palaces for himself, a Winter Palace in the south in modern [Froggy Island](/world/bauhinia/froggy-island/), and a Summer Palace in the north in modern day [Sylvandia](/world/sylvandia/).

### Calendar system

TIR uses the Georgian calendar. Right, instead of anything that makes more sense given the 24 solar terms they canonically have. Meta reason is that I can reference my calendar apps for dates and such and it's easier, and that TIR dates can map perfectly to our dates so I can celebrate them in real life (which I don't do). In-universe reason is that there is no reason. The months especially, since there's no lunar cycle, months won't even make sense. ## Voidspace

What if you fall over the edge of the world? You die. No one had explored voidspace and came back to tell the tale. Every submarine, every task force, every hapless tourist, consumed by the vast blue emptiness below where the visibility is less than a dozen meters below the lowest island and having people lose sight of you means they'll never see you again. You can lean over the railing and watch the sun rise up from below you (well because the sun and moon revolves around the world too why the fuck not), but you must take care to not fall.

Modern science has found that the voidspace mostly consists of an unidentifiable gas they named "void gas." It vaporizes everything it touches, and assimilates the resulting vapor into itself. It's impossible to study it or utilize it, as it would just burn through any vessel that tries to contain it. The most people could do was just measuring how far down does void gas start becoming deadly, by dropping a long measuring tape over the edge, pulling it back up, and looking at where the tape got cut off.

The only things that aren't affected by voidspace are the sun and the moon. No one knows why.

Void gas may be the only unsurmountable barrier to science, and it's clear that voidspace would like to stay that way.
