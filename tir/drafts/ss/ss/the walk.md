By the time I met Sugarcane, I was completely certain we would be late.

"Quick, quick!" I gave her a hug and tugged her forward.

Sugarcane glanced at her watch. "It's only a fifteen-minute walk. We have plenty of time."

"You're right," I said. "I'm just so excited!"

"Me too," she nodded. "My parents really wanted this."

We reached the intersection where the school sat. It was crowded with cars honking at each other and parents waving their children goodbye. The street seemed significantly older, the tiles beneath our feet cracked and uneven because of the tree roots growing under them and protruding out of the ground. We brushed aside the dangling tree whiskers and attempted to avoid tripping over the roots as we made our way to the front gate.

Some students were dragging huge suitcases around, the wheels bouncing on top of the tree roots. "How come they brought suitcases?" I wondered out loud.

"Probably high schoolers," Sugarcane replied. "They stay in dorms."

"There's a high school portion?"

"Yeah," Sugarcane nodded.

"Are we going to attend that, too?"

"That's the plan. For me at least."

A traffic police officer guided us over the pedestrian crossing, and we safely made it to the front gate. It was a large campus, surrounded by barbed wires mounted on gray walls. Tall, foreboding buildings loomed down at us, with barred windows and paint peeling off. I lived in Flower City my whole life and I must've passed this building many times, but it had never occurred to me that this place was a school.

A tall girl with a long ponytail stood in front of the open gate, holding a sign that said "NEW STUDENTS WELCOME." Her ears were black, instead of the usual white; and with her thin face and yellow eyes, she looked like a cat.

We walked over and she eagerly waved at us. "Hello! Are you seventh graders?"

I nodded.

"Welcome to Spy School! I'm Jewel and I'm here to guide new students." She flipped the sign down and revealed a clipboard on the other side. "What are your names?"

"Harmony and Sugarcane," Sugarcane answered.

Jewel's hand traced down the list of names on the clipboard until she arrived at ours. "Harmony… Sugarcane. You're both in class five! That would be at the very right side of that building, on the first floor. Walk in and turn right. You'll see the sign."

"Thanks!" I said as Jewel waved us goodbye.

To the right was a small garden with some basic shrubs and benches with a tree in the middle that was probably a bauhinia tree. A row of classroom doors faced the garden, and we quickly found class five at the very end.

The classroom was only half-filled.
