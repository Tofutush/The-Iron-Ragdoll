It was a Sunday afternoon. I was curled up on the sofa with a book when there came a knock on the door. Dad went to get it.

A man in a jet-black uniform stood in the doorway, sporting two blue horns and a welcoming smile, hands held behind his back. Dad nearly slammed the door right back into his face, but the man reached out a hand and stopped it.

"Wh-What did I do?" Dad's voice was trembling as if he saw a spider in a corner.

"Why, Mr. Entity, if there's anything you did, that was raising a wonderful daughter! Is little Harmony home?"

His eyes locked with mine for a split second. I pretended not to notice, sinking back into my book, though my attention was still focused on him. Black uniform, purple boots, and the shiny silver badge on the hat — the man was from the Ministry of State Security. What did he want with me?

Dad turned around to face me, looking relieved that the officer wasn't coming for him. "Harm? It's for you."

I put down the book and made a face.

"Actually," said the man, "is Mrs. Frosting home? I would love to discuss this matter with the whole family."

Dad nodded solemnly. "Darling!" he called.

Mom came out from her study. "What is it?" And then she saw the man in the doorway. "Oh. What's the matter, officer? Come inside."

We sat down around the tea table. The man introduced himself as Sky Elmwood Dazzle. Mom made tea.

"I am here on behalf of the Middle School for Young Officers in Training Affiliated to the Ministry of State Security." Sky began. "I know it's a mouthful. We usually call it Ministry Affiliate. Since you are about to graduate elementary school, we are giving you an offer to attend middle school here."

"Really?" Mom burst out.

"You are not mistaken. Our administration sees potential in a student like you."

He looked straight at me with a warm smile on his face. Potential? What potential did he see in me?

"What potential?" Mom echoed my question, but in a tone full of suspicion.

Sky let out a laugh. "Have some faith. We looked at your elementary school grades up to this point, and you have been performing spectacularly. So tell me, Harmony, do you want to attend Ministry Affiliate?"

Of course I wanted to attend. Everyone knew how prestigious the school was, and everyone wanted to attend. Which was a little ironic considering its secrecy, but unsurprisingly, the school had done a great job concealing everything about it to the public. Everyone wanted to attend Ministry Affiliate, but nobody knew what it entailed.

I wanted to know. But I couldn't, because I promised Sugarcane I would go to the same school as her.

"I'm sorry, but I'm supposed to go to Flower City Middle School with my friend," I said.

"Sugarcane? I just visited her yesterday. She agreed to attend."

I caught my parents eyeing each other.

"…I can see why," Mom said finally. "Sugarcane is a bright kid."

I coughed.

"Of course, you're amazing too! That's why uncle Sky came to you too!" Mom added immediately with no genuineness in her voice.

"I'm going wherever Sugarcane is going," I said.

"Do we have time to consider?" Dad asked.

"I don't need to consider!" I said. "If Sugarcane is going, then you can count me in, Mr. Sky!"

"I like your enthusiasm," Sky said. "Welcome to Ministry Affiliate!"

"Wait, wait, wait," Mom butted in. "She's in? Can't we get some more time to consider?"

"The decision is up to Harmony herself to make," Sky explained to Mom, but turned to address me again. "We *chose* you. If you don't turn it down yourself, we will assume you will be attending."

"She doesn't even know what you do in that school," Mom frowned. She kept talking about me in third person, as if I weren't there.

"That's the paradox, you see," Sky said apologetically. "If we tell you what you would expect from the school, and you turn it down, that would be a breach of confidentiality on my part. To avoid that, you have to make the decision with all the information you have right now."

"Which is none," Mom said with concern in her voice. Dad reached out a hand to clutch her arm, like he was telling her to calm down. But that was unnecessary, because Mom *had* been calm. She was just ignoring my opinions as usual.

"I do have *some* info," I announced.

"What?" Mom asked.

"Sugarcane is going!"

Mom narrowed her eyes at me. "Don't you want to talk to Sugarcane first to see if she even *wants* to go with you?"

There was a moment of silence as I thought about how to react to that. I settled on, "why would she *not*?"

Mom started to say something, but Sky interrupted her. He did it so naturally, though, that I didn't even mind.

"The decision is yours to make," he said.

Mom looked like she still wanted to say something, but Dad stopped her with a tap on her shoulder.

I stared at Sky and nodded. "Will you be my teacher?"

"Yes," he smiled so reassuringly that I felt like everything would be alright as long as he was there. He held out a hand, and I shook it, squeezing his hand tight the way adults did.

"Keep your grades up, and don't go around telling everyone I visited," Sky warned me. "Thanks for the tea."

He took a curt bow and left.

I sat back down on the sofa and picked my book up. Mom started washing the teacups. "Didn't even take off his boots," she muttered. Dad glared at her and made a shushing noise.

"Actually," I said, "I'm going to find Sugarcane."

"You better do that," Mom said, even though no one asked. "Ask her if she really got recruited into Ministry Affiliate too."

I looked out the window to make sure Sky was well gone before I headed out. As cool as he was, he was still quite intimidating. As soon as I was sure coast was clear, I skipped down the stairs and ran to our usual playground.
