The night before school started, I nearly couldn't fall asleep. I kept tossing and turning as excitement overwhelmed me. I wished that time would go quicker and it could be morning soon, but then I realized that actually falling asleep was the best way to skip time quickly, so I brought all my attention onto trying to fall asleep, which seemed to have the opposite effect. I couldn't remember when I finally drifted into unconsciousness, but I woke with a start when my alarm rang.

Seven-thirty. I had set it to six. What? Who did this?

I dashed out of bed and pulled on my school uniform, which I had took out of the wardrobe last night. Then I threw my backpack, also ready since last night, onto my back, and dashed out of my room.

Mom was in the kitchen. "Morning, Harm," she said.

"Stop calling me that," I said. "Also the weirdest thing happened. I could've sworn my alarm clock was set for six. I'm gonna be late for school!"

"Class starts at nine today," Mom said. "First day. There's no need to hurry."

"Mom! I'm going to be *late* and it's gonna be *all your fault*!" I pulled out my shoes and tried putting them on while standing up. It was hard but I didn't know what I was expecting. I lost balance and bonked my head against the shoe shelf. The family photo fell to the ground with a clatter.

Mom dashed out of the kitchen in a hurry, bent down, picked up the photo, and turned it over in her hands, checking for damage. I stood up slowly, one foot still halfway in the shoe. Only after she set the photo back on the shelf did she extend her arm to me, intending to help me stand.

"Oh, you got it," she said. "Don't do that again; you'll wake Dad."

"He deserves it," I said.

"Alright, young lady. Sit down at the table and finish your breakfast."

The eggs were still burning hot, but I tried swallowing them as quickly as possible and ended up choking myself on the yolk.

"Slow down," Mom said. She was still peeling hers.

"I can't keep Sugarcane waiting," I said.

"Sugarcane is probably still in bed," Mom said, "like a normal person."

"I said I would meet her in front of her apartment."

"There's no hurry," Mom repeated.
